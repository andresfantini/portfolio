The challenge

Estimating delivery times is a practical analytics challenge: inaccurate forecasts affect customer expectations and logistics planning. The Olist dataset contains related information about orders, customers, sellers, products and shipping across Brazil. Delivery times vary across routes and product characteristics, so a single historical average provides limited guidance for individual orders.

Approach & contribution

I contributed to the technical analysis and the conceptual direction of the project, including early data exploration and continued methodological input to the team. The group integrated the marketplace data, engineered logistics-related variables and compared regression models to estimate delivery times.

  • Explored delivery-time distributions, anomalies, missing values and relationships among order and shipping variables.
  • Worked with preparation and integration of tabular data from orders, customers, sellers, products and geolocation sources.
  • Analyzed relevant features such as geographic distance (Haversine), freight value, product weight and shipping times.
  • Contributed to the interpretation of the analytical approach and team model comparisons involving Linear Regression, Decision Trees, Random Forest and LightGBM.
A useful delivery forecast links predictive error to practical decisions: clearer delivery promises, prioritization and better customer communication.

What this demonstrates

The project demonstrates exploratory data analysis, multi-table data preparation, feature engineering, familiarity with regression-model evaluation and the ability to connect analytical findings with business questions. It complements my industrial background with applied Data Science in an e-commerce setting.

Evidence & scope

Collaborative academic work based on publicly available Olist data. The team documented predictive-model results and operational recommendations; no production deployment or measured logistics savings at Olist are claimed.
