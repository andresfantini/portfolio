The challenge

A rural community needed to remotely monitor the environmental and soil conditions of a greenhouse, and control irrigation and ventilation devices. The initial challenge consisted of integrating sensors, actuators, and communication systems to enable remote monitoring and control. Subsequently, the project evolved into a second challenge: structuring, validating, and storing the data to facilitate its historical analysis.

Approach & contribution

The project began in 2023, during a Diploma in Internet of Things at UTN, through a collaborative technological development experience for a rural community. Subsequently, I resumed this domain to develop a Data Engineering solution with Python and PySpark.

  • I designed the IoT architecture with DHT22, TSL2561, and capacitive soil-moisture sensors, ESP32, Raspberry Pi, MQTT/Mosquitto, Node-RED, and Blynk, with irrigation and ventilation actuators.
  • I modeled devices, zones, sensor and actuator events, and prepared simulated data and external historical weather information.
  • I implemented Python/PySpark Bronze → Silver batch pipelines with explicit schemas, separate readers and writers, and dataset-specific quality checks.
  • I persisted validated records to Parquet, added execution/error logging, and documented the modules and processing sequences.

What this demonstrates

The project combines an understanding of IoT hardware and communications with data modeling, PySpark processing, data-quality validation, logging, and modular software design. It demonstrates a technical progression from physical monitoring to preparing structured datasets for analysis.

Evidence & scope

The repository documents five Bronze → Silver pipelines developed with Python and PySpark, with successful executions recorded during development and data persistence in Parquet. To test the processing, sensor and actuator events were used, complemented with external historical weather information. The project also includes documentation and architecture diagrams.
