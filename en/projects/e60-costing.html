The challenge

A small industrial workshop needed a costing system tailored to its E-60 wind-turbine manufacturing process, allowing it to determine production costs, set selling prices and profitability margins, calculate break-even points, and evaluate planning scenarios.

This required mapping production activities, studying movements and work times, identifying consumed resources, and establishing reliable criteria to calculate and monitor costs.

Approach & contribution

The challenge consisted of understanding the production process, designing the economic model that serves as the engine for the costing system, and transforming it into a traceable and maintainable software application. I mapped the production process, its movements and work times, and designed an integrated costing system initially implemented in Excel. I later transformed it into a Python, PostgreSQL, and Streamlit application, applying data modeling, software architecture, and automated tests.

  • Costing system design: I structured activities, resources, times, and criteria for allocating materials, direct labor, and manufacturing overhead.
  • Industrial budgeting: I determined activity levels from the sales budget and prepared production, direct-materials, direct-labor, and manufacturing-overhead budgets.
  • Economic modeling: I integrated budgets and determined unit, fixed and variable costs, contribution margins and break-even points, generating economic and financial information to plan resources, set prices and assess profitability.
  • Software engineering: I transformed the Excel model into a Python/PostgreSQL application using modular architecture, separation of responsibilities, and sound development practices to facilitate maintenance, evolution, and scalability.
  • Traceability and control: I implemented versioned costing and recalculation runs, execution history, idempotency, access controls, and transactional integrity.
  • Interface and quality: I developed an interactive Streamlit interface, backup and recovery procedures, and automated tests, with 1,106 passing tests in version 1.0.
